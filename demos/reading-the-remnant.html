Reading the Remnant

A binary black hole merger radiates one chirp, but the black hole it leaves behind rings in many tones at once — each one fixed entirely by the remnant's mass and spin. If general relativity is right, every tone has to agree on the same two numbers. That agreement is the whole game.

Built after Brian Seymour's lovely Reading Spin from Strain, which does the same kind of dragging–and–looking for the inspiral. This is the ringdown half: instead of one waveform shape encoding spin, you get a discrete spectrum — a Kerr black hole's quasinormal modes — encoding the remnant's mass and spin twice over, once per mode.

The spectrum

Six quasinormal modes of the same remnant — same Mf, same χf — each with its own frequency and decay rate. In GR, moving any one point off its curve means moving them all.

The ringing horizon

The same six modes, drawn as a shape instead of a spectrum — the remnant's horizon settling down, mode by mode, at extreme slow motion.

Surface displacement = ∑ (mode amplitude, from the panel above) × exp(−t/τ) cos(2πft) × that mode's real angular shape (associated Legendre functions). Exaggerated roughly 100× for visibility — a real horizon's ringdown distortion is a far smaller fraction of its radius.
